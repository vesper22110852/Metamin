Decoupling polarization response in a structural color cavity.

A meta-mirror-assisted optical cavity enables one polarization to explore a broad color space while the orthogonal polarization remains locked to a stable output.

The design problem: polarization-selective structural color is often produced through in-plane geometric anisotropy. The same geometry then influences both orthogonal polarization channels, making their spectra difficult to control independently.

The central idea

The study introduces a meta-mirror-assisted cavity in which lateral meta-atom geometry and vertical cavity behavior do not play identical roles for the two polarization states. That asymmetry is the useful feature.

Under x-polarized illumination, reflected color is strongly sensitive to the short-axis radius of the anisotropic meta-atoms, producing diverse and continuously tunable colors. Under y-polarized illumination, the spectrum remains largely insensitive to changes in both lateral radii, producing a polarization-locked color.

Decoupling is more than producing different colors. It is making one optical channel change while another refuses to follow.

Why vertical engineering matters

A purely lateral explanation would be incomplete. The decoupled behavior remains present across changes in cavity thickness, pointing to the stabilizing role of the vertical optical architecture.

Field distributions provide the physical distinction. For x-polarization, localization near the meta-atom tips indicates strong near-field coupling. For y-polarization, a vertically extended cavity field and confinement near the SiO₂ gap spacer support a coupled Fabry–Pérot and gap-surface-plasmon interpretation.

How the claim is tested

  • Map reflected color across both lateral radii rather than showing a few favorable geometries.
  • Track the response as the polarization angle moves from 0° to 90°.
  • Quantify color diversity using the maximum CIEDE2000 perceptual color difference.
  • Inspect resonant electric-field distributions to distinguish the operating mechanisms.
  • Vary cavity thickness to test whether the behavior survives vertical perturbation.

What this enables

The architecture suggests a robust strategy for polarization-selective structural coloration. A tunable channel and a locked channel can serve different information roles, creating opportunities in secure optical encoding, information multiplexing, and multifunctional photonic devices.
